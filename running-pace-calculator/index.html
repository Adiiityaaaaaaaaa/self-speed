Running pace calculator

Runners think in pace — minutes per kilometre. Speedometers think in speed — kilometres per hour. They are the same information inverted, and the conversion is one division:

pace in min/km = 60 ÷ speed in km/h

So 12 km/h is exactly 5:00 per km. 10 km/h is exactly 6:00. Those two are worth memorising, because everything else can be estimated from them.

Pace does not hold across distances

Your sustainable pace drops as distance rises. A runner racing 5K at 5:00/km should expect roughly:

  • 10K — 5:10 to 5:15/km
  • Half marathon — 5:25 to 5:30/km
  • Marathon — 5:45 to 6:00/km

Starting a marathon at half-marathon pace is the most common and most punishing mistake in distance running. The first 30 km feel fine. The last 12 do not.

Converting in your head

km/h to min/km: divide 60 by the speed. Anchor on 10 km/h = 6:00/km, then adjust — 12 km/h is a fifth faster, so 5:00.

min/km to min/mile: multiply by 1.609. A useful shortcut is add 60% — 5:00/km becomes roughly 8:03/mile.

Marathon shortcut: your marathon time in hours is roughly your pace in min/km multiplied by 0.703. At 6:00/km that gives 4.22 hours, or about 4:13.

Measuring pace on the run

GPS pace is noisy at the instant level. Position error of a few metres becomes a large apparent pace swing when sampled every second, which is why watches show you 4:30 one moment and 6:10 the next on steady effort.

Self Speed averages over 2.5 seconds to suppress that, and reports a moving average that excludes time spent stopped. See running speed tracker and average running speed.
