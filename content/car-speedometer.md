# Car speedometer

Your car's speedometer is lying to you, and it is legally required to. Open Self
Speed on a phone in the passenger seat and you will see a number several percent
lower than the dashboard. Here is why, and what to do with that information.

## Your dashboard reads high on purpose

Under **UNECE Regulation 39**, which applies across the EU, UK, India and most of
the world, a car's speedometer must never indicate *less* than the true speed.
It may indicate more, up to a limit:

> The indicated speed must not be lower than the true speed, and must not exceed
> it by more than 10% + 4 km/h.

At a true 100 km/h, a legal speedometer can read anything from 100 to 114 km/h.
Manufacturers deliberately aim high — nobody wants warranty claims from drivers
booked for speeding on an under-reading dial. A 3–7% over-read is typical.

| True speed | Legal indicated range | Typical dashboard |
|---|---|---|
| 50 km/h | 50 – 59 km/h | 52 – 54 |
| 80 km/h | 80 – 92 km/h | 83 – 86 |
| 100 km/h | 100 – 114 km/h | 104 – 107 |
| 120 km/h | 120 – 136 km/h | 125 – 129 |

## Tyres change the reading too

A car speedometer counts driveshaft or wheel rotations and multiplies by an
assumed rolling circumference. Anything that changes that circumference shifts
every reading:

- **Worn tyres** are smaller, so the wheel turns more often per kilometre and
  the dial reads higher still — up to about 2% on fully worn rubber.
- **Under-inflation** has the same effect, slightly.
- **Larger aftermarket wheels** read *low*, which is the dangerous direction and
  in many places makes the car non-compliant.

GPS measures your movement over the ground and is immune to all of it.

## So which one do I obey?

**The dashboard.** Two reasons, and neither is about which is more accurate:

1. Enforcement cameras and police equipment measure true speed, but your margin
   for error is theirs, not yours. Driving to a GPS reading of exactly the limit
   means your true speed is at the limit with no buffer.
2. GPS fails. Tunnels, urban canyons, multi-storey car parks and heavy cloud all
   degrade the fix. A speedometer that occasionally stops working is not
   something to steer by.

Use GPS to *understand* your dashboard's offset, then drive by the dashboard.

## Safety

Set it running before you move off, mount the phone where you do not have to
look down, and never interact with it while driving. If you want speed while
driving, a passenger should hold the phone, or use your car's head-up display.

## Checking your speedometer error

On a quiet straight road with a clear sky, hold a steady indicated speed and
read the GPS number. Repeat at 50, 80 and 100 km/h. The difference is your car's
offset, and it stays roughly proportional across the range — useful to know on
long motorway drives where a consistent few km/h adds up.

## What speed cameras actually measure

Understanding the enforcement side explains why the dashboard margin exists.

**Radar and lidar** measure your true speed directly — radar via Doppler shift
off the vehicle body, lidar by timing laser pulses. Both are calibrated
instruments subject to periodic certification.

**Average speed cameras** photograph a numberplate at two points a known distance
apart and divide. This is the same method Self Speed falls back on, and it is
immune to braking for the camera.

**Induction loops** cut into the road surface detect a vehicle passing over two
coils in sequence.

All of them measure true speed, not indicated speed. Most jurisdictions apply an
enforcement tolerance on top — commonly the greater of 2 mph or 10 percent — but
that tolerance is a matter of policy, not entitlement, and it can be withdrawn.

## Why a GPS reading is not a defence

People occasionally hope a phone screenshot will contest a ticket. It will not.

A calibrated enforcement device has a documented certification chain, a
maintenance record and a legal status as an approved measuring instrument. A
consumer phone has none of these. Its accuracy figure is the receiver's own
estimate, not a certification, and the reading depends on satellite conditions
nobody recorded at the time.

Use GPS to understand your car, not to argue with anyone.

## Frequently asked

### Why is my GPS speed lower than my speedometer?

Because your speedometer is required by law never to read low, so manufacturers
build in a margin. A 3 to 7 percent over-read is normal and correct. Both
instruments are working.

### Which is the real speed?

GPS is closer to your true speed over the ground, assuming a good satellite fix.
But drive by the dashboard — GPS drops out in tunnels and cities, and it has no
legal standing.

### Does GPS work inside a car?

Yes, through the windscreen, which is usually the best-placed glass. Heated
windscreens with embedded filaments and some heat-reflective glazing attenuate
the signal noticeably. A dashboard mount near the screen works better than a
phone in a cupholder or door pocket.

### Will changing my wheels affect it?

It will affect your dashboard but not the GPS. Larger wheels make the dashboard
read low, which is the dangerous direction and in many places makes the vehicle
non-compliant. GPS is unaffected because it never touches the drivetrain.

### Does it work in a tunnel?

No. GPS needs line of sight to satellites and a tunnel has none. The reading goes
grey and resumes after a short reacquisition delay on the far side.
