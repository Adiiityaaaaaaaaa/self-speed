## The formula

> km/h = mph × 1.609344

One international mile is exactly 1,609.344 metres — a definition fixed in 1959
when the US, UK, Canada, Australia, New Zealand and South Africa agreed to
standardise the inch at exactly 25.4 mm. Before that, the US and UK miles
differed very slightly.

## Doing it in your head

**Add half, then a tenth.** This approximates 1.6 and is accurate to about
0.6 percent:

- 60 mph → 60 + 30 + 6 = 96 km/h (true 96.6)
- 70 mph → 70 + 35 + 7 = 112 km/h (true 112.7)
- 30 mph → 30 + 15 + 3 = 48 km/h (true 48.3)

The rough version — **multiply by 1.5** — is quicker but under-reads by
7 percent, which is too much to be useful near a speed limit.

Two traps for British and American drivers on the continent:

1. **A 130 sign is not 130 mph.** It is 130 km/h, or 81 mph.

## Why three countries still use miles

The United Kingdom, the United States and Liberia are the only countries where
road signs are posted in miles per hour. The reasons are political rather than
technical.

The US formally adopted the metric system as its preferred system in the **Metric
Conversion Act of 1975** — but the act made conversion voluntary, and public
resistance to changing road signs killed the road programme within four years.
American science, medicine and the military all went metric; the roads did not.

The UK began converting in 1965 and got most of the way there. Distances on
official documents, fuel, food and construction are all metric. Road signs were
scheduled for conversion in 1973, then repeatedly deferred on grounds of cost,
and the programme was formally abandoned. The result is a country that buys
petrol in litres to drive miles.

Liberia's usage descends from close historical ties to the United States.

## Frequently asked

### What is 60 mph in km/h?

96.6 km/h. This is the UK national speed limit for single carriageways, which is
why continental drivers in Britain often see 97 on a metric dashboard and assume
they have misread a sign.

### Is 100 mph fast?

161 km/h. It is above the motorway limit of essentially every country in the
world. Germany's unrestricted autobahn sections are the main exception, and even
there the advisory limit is 130 km/h, or 81 mph.

### How do I convert quickly without a calculator?

Add half, then add a tenth. For 70 mph: 70 + 35 + 7 = 112, against a true 112.7.
It is accurate to under 1 percent, which is good enough for reading a road sign.
