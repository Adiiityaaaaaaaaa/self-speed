# Average running speed

**A recreational runner averages 8–10 km/h, or roughly 6:00–7:30 per km.** A
well-trained club runner sits nearer 12–14 km/h. Elite marathoners hold about
20 km/h for two hours, which is the part most people fail to appreciate.

Runners think in pace — minutes per kilometre — rather than speed, so both are
given throughout.

## By ability

| Runner | km/h | min/km | min/mile |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beginner, run-walk | 6.0 – 7.5 | 8:00 – 10:00 | 12:53 – 16:06 |
| Recreational, steady | 8.0 – 10.0 | 6:00 – 7:30 | 9:39 – 12:04 |
| Regular, fit | 10.0 – 12.0 | 5:00 – 6:00 | 8:03 – 9:39 |
| Club runner | 12.0 – 14.0 | 4:17 – 5:00 | 6:54 – 8:03 |
| Competitive amateur | 14.0 – 16.0 | 3:45 – 4:17 | 6:02 – 6:54 |
| Elite marathon | 19.0 – 21.0 | 2:51 – 3:09 | 4:35 – 5:04 |
| World record 100 m (peak) | 44.7 | 1:20 | 2:09 |

That last row is peak instantaneous speed, not something anyone sustains — Usain
Bolt's 9.58 s record averages 37.6 km/h over the full 100 m, with a peak near
44.7 km/h around the 60 m mark.

## Pace fades with distance

The single most useful thing to understand about running speed is that it is not
one number. The same runner's sustainable pace drops predictably as distance
rises:

| Distance | Typical fade from 5K pace |
|---|---|
| 1 mile | about 5% faster |
| 5K | baseline |
| 10K | 3 – 5% slower |
| Half marathon | 8 – 10% slower |
| Marathon | 15 – 20% slower |

So a runner who races 5K at 5:00/km would expect roughly 5:15/km for 10K,
5:25/km for a half, and 5:45–6:00/km for a marathon. Runners who ignore this and
start a marathon at half-marathon pace produce the sport's most reliable
disaster.

## Realistic finishing times

For an ordinary recreational runner, not an elite:

| Distance | Beginner | Recreational | Fit | Competitive |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5K | 35:00 | 28:00 | 22:00 | 18:00 |
| 10K | 1:12 | 58:00 | 46:00 | 38:00 |
| Half marathon | 2:40 | 2:10 | 1:45 | 1:25 |
| Marathon | 5:30 | 4:30 | 3:40 | 3:00 |

A sub-4-hour marathon is a common aspirational target and represents roughly
5:41/km, or 10.5 km/h.

## What changes your pace

**Heat and humidity.** Larger than most people allow for. Above about 20 °C
performance degrades measurably, and humidity compounds it by blocking
evaporative cooling. A hot marathon can cost 10–20 minutes against the same
fitness in cool conditions.

**Elevation.** Both altitude (thinner air, less oxygen) and gradient. A rule of
thumb many runners use is 10–20 seconds per km slower for every 1% of average
gradient.

**Surface.** Trail running is substantially slower than road for the same
effort — uneven footing, obstacles and gradient combine. Comparing trail pace to
road pace is close to meaningless.

**Fatigue and training state.** Obvious, but worth stating: pace on tired legs
mid-training-block is not a measure of fitness.

## Why GPS pace jumps around

If your watch or phone swings from 4:30 to 6:10 on steady effort, that is the
GPS, not you. Position error of a few metres translates into a large apparent
pace change when sampled every second.

Self Speed averages over 2.5 seconds, which removes most of the jitter while
still responding quickly to genuine changes. Total distance stays accurate
because the random errors cancel over time.

**Tracks are the exception.** A 400 m track is the worst possible GPS
environment — tight continuous bends where the systematic error is comparable to
the lane width. For track sessions, use a stopwatch. See
[running speed tracker](/running-speed-tracker/).
