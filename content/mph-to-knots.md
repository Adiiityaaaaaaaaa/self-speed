## The formula

> knots = mph ÷ 1.150779
> mph = knots × 1.150779

The factor is the ratio of the two miles. A statute mile is 1,609.344 m; a
nautical mile is exactly 1,852 m. Divide one by the other and you get 1.150779.

## Two different miles, and why

The **statute mile** descends from the Roman *mille passus*, a thousand double
paces. English law fixed it at eight furlongs in 1593, giving the memorably
awkward 5,280 feet.

The **nautical mile** is geometric rather than historical: it is one minute of
latitude. The Earth's circumference divided by 360 degrees of 60 minutes gives
roughly 1,852 m.

That definition is why the unit survives at sea. On a chart, one minute of
latitude on the side scale *is* one nautical mile, so a navigator measures
distance with dividers against the latitude scale and never converts anything.
No other unit has that property, which is why two centuries of metrication have
failed to displace it.

## Which unit gets used where

| Domain | Unit | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Road vehicles, UK and US | mph | Legal requirement on signs |
| Road vehicles, elsewhere | km/h | Metric standard |
| Ships and boats | knots | Charts are in nautical miles |
| Aircraft | knots | Aviation charts share marine geometry |
| Wind, marine forecasts | knots | Beaufort scale is defined in knots |
| Wind, land forecasts | km/h or mph | Matches local road units |
| Physics and engineering | m/s | SI unit |

## Speed over ground versus through water

If you are converting because you are on a boat, the more important distinction
is what is being measured. GPS gives **speed over ground**; a paddlewheel log
gives **speed through water**. The difference between them is the current — see
[boat speedometer](/boat-speedometer-knots/) and
[knots to km/h](/knots-to-kmh/).

Self Speed reads out in knots and reports distance in nautical miles when you
select those units.
